# H5N1 Avian Influenza Research Papers

A curated collection of scientific literature on H5N1 highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI), focusing on host genomics, cross-species transmission, and pandemic preparedness.

---

## Executive Summary

The H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b virus has emerged as a significant global threat, spreading across all continents since 2021. Unlike previous H5N1 outbreaks largely confined to poultry, this clade has demonstrated unprecedented ability to cross species barriers, infecting mammals including dairy cattle, seals, and humans. This document summarizes key scientific literature tracking this evolution.

---

## Part 1: Host Genomics and Species Barriers

### ANP32 Proteins - The Key to Species Specificity

**Long JS, et al. (2019)**
*Species-specific differences in ANP32 proteins regulate avian influenza virus polymerase activity*
eLife. DOI: [10.7554/eLife.45066](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.45066)

**Summary:** ANP32 proteins are essential host factors for influenza virus replication. Avian ANP32A contains a 33-amino-acid insertion that supports avian influenza polymerase activity, while mammalian ANP32 proteins lack this insertion. This molecular difference is a primary barrier to cross-species transmission.

**Cited for:** ANP32A/B species-specific differences explaining why avian flu doesn't easily infect mammals.

---

**Long JS, et al. (2024)**
*Receptor binding specificity of recent H5N1 influenza viruses associated with avian-to-human transmission*
Nature Reviews Microbiology. DOI: [10.1038/s41579-024-01015-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41579-024-01015-2)

**Summary:** Comprehensive review of how H5N1 viruses are adapting their receptor binding to potentially improve human infectivity. Discusses the shift from avian-type (α2,3-linked sialic acid) to human-type (α2,6-linked) receptor binding.

**Cited for:** Review of ANP32 proteins and species barriers in context of recent outbreaks.

---

**Nature Communications (2023)**
*Creating resistance to avian influenza infection through genome editing of the ANP32 gene family*
Nature Communications. DOI: [10.1038/s41467-023-41476-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41476-3)

**Summary:** Demonstrated that editing ANP32A alone is insufficient—viruses rapidly evolve escape mutations. Triple knockout of ANP32A, ANP32B, and ANP32E is required for robust resistance. The PA-349K/PB2-M631L double mutant virus could replicate even in ANP32A knockout cells.

**Cited for:** Proof of concept for genetic resistance in poultry; shows complexity of host-virus interactions.

### Innate Immunity Differences

**Barber MRW, et al. (2010)**
*Absence of RIG-I leads to a functional replacement by MDA5 in avian species*
Journal of Immunology. DOI: [10.4049/jimmunol.0902471](https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.0902471)

**Summary:** Chickens lack RIG-I, a key viral RNA sensor present in ducks and mammals. This absence is compensated by MDA5, but the different sensing mechanism may contribute to chickens' higher susceptibility to influenza.

**Cited for:** Fundamental differences in avian innate immunity affecting disease outcomes.

---

**Haller O, et al. (2018)**
*MX1 proteins show differential antiviral activity against influenza A viruses*
PLoS Pathogens. DOI: [10.1371/journal.ppat.1007426](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1007426)

**Summary:** MX1 (myxovirus resistance) proteins vary dramatically in antiviral potency across species. Human MxA strongly inhibits influenza, while chicken Mx shows minimal activity. The G/A polymorphism at position 2032 in chicken Mx has been studied but shows inconsistent protective effects.

**Cited for:** Species-specific MX1 antiviral activity differences.

**Journal of Virology (2025)**

## Part 2: Cross-Species Transmission to Mammals

### Dairy Cattle Outbreak (2024)

**mBio (2024)**
*Avian influenza A (H5N1) virus in dairy cattle: origin, evolution, and cross-species transmission*
mBio. DOI: [10.1128/mbio.02542-24](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.02542-24)

**Summary:** First comprehensive analysis of H5N1 in US dairy cattle. The Texas-origin viruses share nearly identical genome sequences and belong to clade 2.3.4.4b genotype B3.13. As of late 2024, the virus has spread to cattle in 17 states, affecting over 1,000 dairy herds.

**Cited for:** Origin and spread of the unprecedented cattle outbreak.

---

**bioRxiv (2024)**
*From birds to mammals: spillover of highly pathogenic avian influenza H5N1 virus to dairy cattle led to efficient intra- and interspecies transmission*
bioRxiv. [PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.22.595317.full.pdf)

**Summary:** Documents how the spillover from birds to cattle has enabled efficient mammal-to-mammal transmission within cattle populations, raising concerns about viral adaptation.

---

**Emerging Infectious Diseases (2024)**
*Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza A(H5N1) Clade 2.3.4.4b Virus Infection in Domestic Dairy Cattle and Cats, United States, 2024*
Emerg Infect Dis. PMC: [PMC11210653](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11210653/)

**Summary:** Reports infections in both cattle and cats at US farms, demonstrating the virus's ability to infect multiple mammalian species in agricultural settings.

### Marine Mammal Outbreaks

**bioRxiv (2024)**
*Massive outbreak of Influenza A H5N1 in elephant seals at Península Valdés, Argentina: increased evidence for mammal-to-mammal transmission*
bioRxiv. [PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.31.596774.full.pdf)

**Summary:** Documents the October 2023 mass mortality event in southern elephant seals. Genomic analysis reveals viruses form a distinct clade with marine mammal viruses from Peru, Chile, Brazil, and Uruguay, all sharing identical mammalian adaptation mutations. Strong evidence for mammal-to-mammal transmission.

**Key finding:** More than 30,000 South American sea lions died as H5N1 spread along Peru/Chile coasts in 2022-2023.

---

**NCBI PMC (2024)**
*Epidemiological data of an influenza A/H5N1 outbreak in elephant seals in Argentina indicates mammal-to-mammal transmission*
PMC: [PMC11555070](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11555070/)

**Summary:** Epidemiological and phylogenetic evidence supporting sustained mammal-to-mammal H5N1 transmission in pinnipeds.

---

**Scientific Reports (2025)**
*Genotype A3 influenza A(H5N1) isolated from fur seals shows high virulence in mammals, but not airborne transmission*
Scientific Reports. DOI: [10.1038/s41598-025-28032-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-28032-3)

**Summary:** Russian fur seal outbreak analysis. Viruses belong to genotype A3 of European origin and harbor mammalian-adaptive substitutions PB2-K482R and NP-N319K. High virulence but no evidence of airborne transmission.

---

**PMC (2024)**
*Increasing spillover of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus to mammals*
PMC: [PMC11382104](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11382104/)

**Summary:** Review of increasing frequency of H5N1 spillover events to diverse mammalian species globally.

## Part 3: Human Infections and Pandemic Risk

### US Human Cases (2024-2025)

**Nature Medicine (2025)**
*Human infections with highly pathogenic avian influenza A(H5N1) viruses in the United States from March 2024 to May 2025*
Nature Medicine. DOI: [10.1038/s41591-025-03905-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03905-2)

**Summary:**
- 70 human cases in US (Nov 2024 - May 2025)
- Exposures: 41 dairy cows, 24 commercial poultry, 2 backyard poultry, 3 unknown
- Symptoms: 89% eye redness, 46% fever, 41% respiratory
- Outcomes: 81% received antivirals, 4 hospitalized, 3 pneumonia, 1 death
- **No human-to-human transmission detected**

**Cited for:** Comprehensive epidemiological data on US human cases.

---

**Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology (2025)**
*Emerging threats of H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b: cross-species transmission, pathogenesis, and pandemic risk*
Frontiers. [Link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2025.1625665/full)

**Summary:** Comprehensive review of pandemic risk. Notes that unlike earlier H5N1 with ~60% mortality, cattle-linked infections show milder presentation. Serosurveys suggest 7-14% of dairy farm workers may have evidence of infection, indicating many undetected cases.

---

**Gene (2025)**
*The emerging pandemic threat of H5N1: Evolutionary adaptations for human transmission, zoonotic spillovers and surveillance gaps*
Gene (ScienceDirect). [Link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378111925005128)

**Summary:** Analysis of evolutionary adaptations that could enable human-to-human transmission, and current surveillance gaps.

**Journal of Virology (2024)**

J Virol. DOI: [10.1128/jvi.02209-24](https://doi.org/10.1128/jvi.02209-24)

**Summary:** Historical perspective from 1997 Hong Kong outbreak (33% CFR) through current situation. 878 human H5N1 cases reported 2003-2023, mostly in Southeast Asia and Egypt.

**Summary:** Current clade shows better adaptation to ducks than chickens. IFITM genes substantially upregulated in duck lung/ileum following infection, while chickens show minimal IFITM response. Ducks act as silent carriers with longer viral shedding.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Species barriers are eroding**: The ANP32 protein barrier is being circumvented through viral adaptation, evidenced by sustained mammal-to-mammal transmission in seals and cattle.

2. **Cattle represent a new reservoir**: The 2024 US dairy cattle outbreak is unprecedented and has resulted in human infections.

3. **Marine mammal die-offs signal adaptation**: Over 30,000 sea lions died in South America, with genomic evidence of mammal-adapted viral lineages.

4. **Human cases are mild but concerning**: Unlike historical 60% mortality, current infections are mostly mild—but this may facilitate undetected transmission and further adaptation.

5. **Surveillance is critical**: Many infections go undetected; serosurveys suggest 7-14% of exposed workers may be infected without knowing.

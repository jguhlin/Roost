**Figure 3. Host-genetic knowledge lags far behind available
genomic resources — and the gaps are immediately tractable.**
(A) All 722 species in the Roost species index (H5N1-detected and
tracked hosts), orders as in (B); within each order, studied species lead
(blue, dark to light by gene-link depth), then genome-available unstudied
(amber), then no genome (red cross): 59 species have ever been named
as a host in typed study-subject claims; 442 — 88.6% of the
499 genome-available species — carry a genome but have never
been a study subject; 221 have no genome (2 studied species lack
one: Lynx lynx · Tyto furcata). 80 threatened species
(IUCN CR/EN/VU/NT) sit in the genome-available unstudied cell, including
*Gymnogyps californianus*, *Aythya baeri*, *Panthera tigris*, *Leucogeranus leucogeranus*, *Grus americana*, *Porphyrio hochstetteri*, each with a chromosome-level assembly; 344 of the 442
are least-concern. This is an index of research opportunity, not a
susceptibility score.
(B) Species per order (teal) against host-genetics study attention (blue)
— 4 of the larger bird orders have zero studied species
(Pelecaniformes, Suliformes, Psittaciformes, Procellariiformes; 99 species combined), and
even within the studied set depth is shallow: 40 of
59 studied species are named by ≤4 genes, while only
6 species carry ≥20 gene links.
(C) Geographic distribution of the no-genome gap: every H5N1 detection of
a no-genome species with coordinates (1,239 records) binned into 2.5°
cells, coloured by distinct species per cell (Pacific-centred). The gap
concentrates along coasts and wetland flyways: hottest cells are the US
Great Plains (34 species), South Africa's Western Cape (21), Patagonia
(18) and south-eastern Brazil (12); 62 countries carry at least one
such species. All numbers are generated from the same artifacts as the
live site (data freeze 2026-10-29).
